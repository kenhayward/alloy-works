**Goal:** a person can give a script a token that does less than they may and stops the moment they
say; an organisation's directory decides who is in which group, and groups hold roles; and an
administrator can see what anybody may do, anywhere.

## Global constraints

- Test titles cite only what they show, checked with `pnpm trace show <ID>`, in a literal title.
- Each test is watched fail: a new test before the code, or, where the code exists, by breaking it.
- No em or en dash in user-facing text, and no real names, addresses or paths in a fixture.
- `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm format`, the affected suites, then `pnpm trace generate` after
  Prettier and `pnpm trace pins`; the full suite before each pull request, and its CI log read.
- Every new route joins the access and cross-tenant harnesses. A bearer credential gets cross-tenant
  cases of its own: a token from one environment is not found in another.
- A token's secret never reaches a log, an error body or a crash report. A test checks the request log.
- Every trigger or function reading a table reads it by `tg_table_schema`, never the search path
  (W11.1's review).

## W12.1: Tokens

1. A migration: `api_token` holding the principal, the name, the scopes as a subset of the closed
   permission set, the hash, the time created, the expiry (required, at most 365 days after creation)
   and the time last used. It is deleted with its principal.
2. The request path reads a bearer before the cookie. The token's scopes join the request's facts, and
   `decide` refuses a permission outside them as `scoped`, reading never.
3. `GET`, `POST /v1/tokens` and `DELETE /v1/tokens/{id}`, the caller's own. A route declares when it takes
   a session alone: the token routes, sign-out and the stream.
4. Tests:
   - IAM-034: scoped below the creator; refused outside the scopes; never above the creator's grants;
     the expiry required and bounded.
   - IAM-035: revoked, then refused at the next request.
   - A token cannot reach a session-only route.
   - Cross-tenant: a token is not found in another environment.
   - The secret is absent from the log.

**W12.1, as built.** Migration 0038 makes `api_token`: the principal, deleted with it; a trimmed name of
1 to 80 characters; the SHA-256 of the secret, 64 hex digits and unique; the scopes, a subset of the
closed permission set without `read` (TK-B: a read scope would be a mask that masks nothing), each once,
checked by a function that reads no table; `created_at`; `expires_at`, required, after `created_at` and
at most 365 days after it; and `last_used_at`. The runtime role inserts the five columns a token is made
from, never `created_at`, updates `last_used_at` alone, and deletes, which is revoking. `AccessFacts` gains
`scopes`, undefined for a session, and `decide` refuses an allowed permission outside them, `read` never,
as `scoped`, after the walk and after the external cap, so the explanation still names the grants and a
refusal the grants make keeps its own reason: a scope never reads as though it could have granted
anything (IAM-062). The request path reads `Authorization` before the cookie. Only the `Bearer` scheme
is read there: any other is passed over and the cookie decides. A `Bearer` that is not `Bearer awt_`
and 43 characters, or a token not found or expired, is `401 unauthenticated`, whatever cookie is sent
with it. A route declaring `credential: 'session'` - signing out, the stream and the three
token routes - answers a found token `403 token_not_allowed`, which reads better than a `401` telling a
script with a working token to sign in; the token is looked up first, so a revoked or foreign one is
`401` everywhere. `authorise` puts the request's scopes into the facts it loads, which masks the route's
decision and every flag a handler decides from `Authorised.facts`; `authoriseAt` takes a `Caller`; the
texts route and `listSpacesFor` take the scopes for the flags they decide from facts of their own; the
readable sets and the explanation of another principal are not masked. The listing of tokens is paged
by id like the invitations, since the listings harness holds every list to a cursor, and `POST /v1/tokens`
takes no idempotency key, since a kept answer would keep the secret for a day. The OpenAPI document
gains a `token` bearer scheme on every authenticated operation but the session-only ones, and the explain
answer's reason gains `scoped`, which the access page words, though explaining another principal never
answers it.

From the final review: the mask in `authoriseAt` had no test, and removing it left the suite green; a
test now has a token scoped to `edit` refused the bytes of an upload Grace's session began, and one
scoped to `create` fill it, and goes red without it. `POST /v1/samples`, which writes a row and queues a
job, now takes a session alone, so a token with no scopes writes nothing; it may still ask for a
preview, decided on `read` (PV-B), and TK-B, the features and the changelog now say so. `listSpacesFor`
takes the scopes as a required parameter ahead of the page, `undefined` for a session, so no caller can
leave them out by forgetting. Any `Authorization` header used to shut out the cookie, so a deployment
behind a reverse proxy asking for `Basic` would have refused every browser: only `Bearer`, in any case,
is read now, and a malformed one is still `401`. `POST /v1/tokens` answers the secret with
`Cache-Control: no-store`.

## W12.2: Tokens on the page

1. The account page's API tokens: list, issue with the scopes and expiry, the secret shown once, revoke.
2. `GET /v1/tokens?principal=` and a revoke for a tenant administrator.
3. Tests: issuing and copying, revoking; an administrator revoking a person's token, anyone else refused.

**W12.2, as built.** The administrator's list and revoke are routes of their own,
`GET /v1/principals/{id}/tokens` and `DELETE /v1/principals/{id}/tokens/{token}`, rather than
`GET /v1/tokens?principal=` and the owner's `DELETE` widened: the contract declares one access per
route, so each declares the one decision it takes - `administer` at the tenant, with a session alone
(TK-D) - where a widened route would decide a session in its contract and `administer` in its handler,
out of sight of the access harness and the published document. The owner's three stay as they were. A
person the environment does not hold is `404`, so another environment's is not told apart from nobody,
and a token not that person's is `404`. The revoke answers `200 { revoked }`, as removing a grant and
withdrawing an invitation do, since a permission-checked handler returns its body after its
transaction commits and has no reply to send a `204` with. Both join the access, cross-tenant and
listing harnesses. There is no account page: the account chip holds a menu, and a modal is never a
route (docs/interface), so **API tokens** is a modal from it, as Administration is. It lists the
person's tokens - name, what each may do in the access page's words with reading first, its expiry and
its last use or `never` - with Revoke on each, asking first in a dialog of its own. **New token** opens
a dialog beside it rather than inside it, so it closes alone and the outer dialog's watch for a late
first field never takes its focus: a name, the scopes as checkboxes under a note that reading is always
allowed, and the expiry as a date from tomorrow to a year away, 90 days unless changed, refused in the
form outside that and sent as the start of the day chosen. The secret is shown in that dialog with Copy and a warning that it will not be shown again,
lives in its state alone and goes however it closes; a test spies on `Storage.prototype.setItem`, the
console and the address. Administration's People gives each person who has signed in **Tokens**, their
list in the same table with Revoke. A refusal is said in the service's `message` where it gave one. The
service tests cite IAM-035 once, where an administrator's revoke is refused at the owner's next request;
the renderer's tests cite nothing, since none shows a token doing or being refused anything.

From the final review: Tokens and Back to people each took away the button that had focus, which fell
to the page, where Escape no longer closed Administration and Tab left it; a person's tokens heading
now takes focus as it opens, and Back returns it to their Tokens button, or to the section's heading
where that is not shown. A year of calendar days is 365 days and an hour where the clocks go back once
more than they go forward in between - in New York from 9 March 2026 to 9 March 2027 - so in the first
hour of such a day the last day offered started more than 365 days off and the service refused it: the
form now refuses by milliseconds from now, as the service does, and offers the day before as its last
there, tested with New York's zone and a fixed clock. The field reads Works until the start of, since a
token stops as its day begins, and the list shows the expiry to the minute under Works until and the
scopes under May do, where May beside dates read as the month. The tests now hold the checkboxes to
exactly the token scopes, with no Read; no Tokens button for somebody invited who has not signed in;
and what is said when the token cannot be copied.

## W12.3: Groups

1. A migration: `identity_provider.groups_claim`, `groups` by default.
2. The group routes: `GET` and `POST /v1/groups`, `PUT /v1/groups/{id}/members` for the environment's own
   groups, and `DELETE /v1/groups/{id}`. A grant may name a group.
3. At an organisation sign-in, the provider's memberships brought into line with the claim, an absent
   claim counting as none.
4. The stand-in provider asserts groups for its invented users.
5. Tests:
   - IAM-009: a member by the claim holds the group's role, and loses it at the next sign-in without the
     value.
   - A provider group cannot be filled by hand.
   - An absent claim empties the memberships.
   - Deleting a group takes its grants.
